How The Light Table Works

A genuine banknote is a cotton-linen sheet with a watermark formed inside the fibre, not printed on the surface. When you put the note on a bright screen, light passes through and the internal structure becomes visible. A photocopied or digitally printed fake has no internal structure — its "watermark" is toner sitting on top of the paper.

TRANSMITTEDLook through the note Watermark, windowed thread, see-through register. Light from behind, eyes in front.
RAKINGLook across the surface Tilt the note near the glowing screen so light skims the surface. Raised intaglio print casts tiny shadows.
TILTRock the note With the board behind, tilt the note. The foil patch and thread shift hue.

What To Look For

MICROPRINTSharp legible letters Zoom to maximum on the microtext band. Genuine lines stay crisp; fakes break into dots or blur.
INTAGLIORaised ink ridges Torch on, held almost parallel to the note. The large numeral and portrait should stand up in relief.
EDGESClean cut, no halo Printed edges on genuine notes are razor-sharp. Inkjet fakes show colour fringing and fuzzy borders.
SUBSTRATECotton fibre, not glossy Invert helps. Real paper has visible irregular fibres; polymer/paper fakes look uniform and plasticky.

2019 Series · Layout

Schematic positions of the main security elements. Not to scale.

01 WATERMARKLion head + electrotype Soft multi-tonal shading inside the paper. Visible from both sides, clearest in transmitted light. Sharp printed outline = fake.
02 FOIL PATCHColour-shift hologram Changes hue as you tilt. Photocopies show a flat grey or muddy copy of the pattern.
03 INTAGLIORaised print Large numeral and portrait sit above the paper. Feel it, or catch it with raking light.
04 THREADWindowed, colour-shift Weaves in and out of the paper. Solid line in transmitted light, dashes where it dips inside.
05 MICROTEXTRazor-sharp lettering Legible at magnification. Blurs or dots into a solid line on a copy.
06 SERIALSTwo, evenly printed Consistent font, spacing and ink depth. Look for a mismatched or misaligned pair.
